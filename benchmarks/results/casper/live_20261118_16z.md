## What it showed

1. **A late cycle outlasts a walltime sized for a normal one.** The job had 1 h 15 min and would have been
   ended by PBS at about 18:10 UTC; F13 arrived at 17:40. The launcher now gives every job enough walltime to
   reach its own give-up time (`walltime_for`), since a job leaves as soon as its run is complete.
2. **One bucket can be well behind the other.** Google had F13 more than four minutes before AWS (it was listed
   there at 17:35 UTC; AWS got the file at 17:39:36). The job reads one bucket, so it waited. Falling back to the other
   bucket for an hour that is late is not implemented.
3. **Start-up.** The stores were created 4 min 18 s after the job started. The first idx reached AWS at about
   the same moment (59.3 minutes), so this run does not show how much of that was start-up and how much was
   waiting; F01 was published 1.2 minutes after its idx, the longest lag of the run.
